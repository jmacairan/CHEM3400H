Part A Multiple Choice

Q1. Which of the following normally contributes the largest share of total measurement variance for a heterogeneous soil?

  1. Instrumental measurement
  2. Field sampling
  3. Sample preparation
  4. Unit conversion
  5. Final data reporting
Solution

B. Field sampling

For a heterogeneous soil, differences among locations and particles usually make field sampling the largest contribution to total measurement variance. Instrumental variance is often much smaller than the variance introduced before the sample reaches the instrument.

Q2. Which of the following best describes a composite sample?

  1. A single grab sample collected at one point in time
  2. A mixture of multiple samples collected across space or time
  3. A random selection of clusters that are sampled completely
  4. A control sample collected to detect contamination
  5. A blank sample prepared in the laboratory
Solution

B. A mixture of multiple samples collected across space or time

A composite sample combines two or more individual samples or increments into one sample for analysis. The result represents an average over the locations or times included in the composite.

Q4. A 95 percent confidence interval for a population mean is 46 to 54 mg per kg. Which statement is correct?

  1. Ninety-five percent of individual results must fall between 46 and 54 mg per kg
  2. The next result has a 95 percent chance of falling between 46 and 54 mg per kg
  3. The interval provides a plausible range for the true population mean
  4. Exactly 95 percent of the population lies in the interval
  5. The interval proves that the sample mean equals the population mean
Solution

C. The interval provides a plausible range for the true population mean

The interval estimates the population mean, not the range containing individual observations. In repeated sampling, approximately 95 percent of intervals constructed by the same procedure would contain the true population mean.

Q6. In the Data Quality Objectives process, which of the following should be defined before choosing sampling locations and sample numbers?

  1. Picking sample locations closest to a Starbucks
  2. The decision, target population, and tolerable error
  3. The easiest locations to access
  4. The final sample labels
  5. The chromatographic stationary phase
Solution

B. The decision, target population, and tolerable error

The sampling design must follow from the decision that the data will support, the population to which the conclusion will apply, and the amount of uncertainty or decision error that can be tolerated.

Q7. Which sample is taken to the site and opened or handled there to check whether contamination was introduced during sample collection?

  1. Trip blank
  2. Field duplicate
  3. Field blank
  4. Calibration standard
  5. Unknown field sample
Solution

C. Field blank

A field blank is exposed to field conditions and handled at the sampling site. Contamination detected in it can indicate contamination introduced during field handling or collection. A trip blank travels with the samples but normally remains unopened.

Q8. At a former battery site, only soil from 0 to 30 cm was sampled even though records suggest buried waste near 80 cm. Which statement best describes the problem?

  1. The sampled depth does not match the target population for the redevelopment decision
  2. The confidence interval is automatically invalid
  3. Too many sample depths were included
  4. The analytical method is necessarily biased high
  5. The samples should all be composited before analysis
Solution

A. The sampled depth does not match the target population for the redevelopment decision

The sampling design excludes the depth where buried contamination may occur. Even precise and analytically correct results from 0–30 cm would not represent the full soil profile relevant to redevelopment.

Q9. For a solute with distribution coefficient K = Cₛ/Cₘ, a larger K generally means the solute:

  1. Spends less time in the stationary phase and elutes earlier
  2. Has no interaction with either phase
  3. Moves at the same speed as the mobile phase
  4. Spends more time in the stationary phase and elutes later
  5. Must be analyzed by gas chromatography
Solution

D. Spends more time in the stationary phase and elutes later

A larger K=Cs/Cm indicates a greater equilibrium concentration in the stationary phase relative to the mobile phase. The solute is therefore retained more strongly and moves through the column more slowly.

Q11. On a C18 reversed-phase stationary phase, retention generally increases with:

  1. Increasing ionic character in every case
  2. Decreasing molecular size regardless of structure
  3. Increasing hydrophobicity
  4. Decreasing detector response
  5. Decreasing injection concentration
Solution

C. Increasing hydrophobicity

A C18 stationary phase is nonpolar. More hydrophobic analytes interact more strongly with it and generally remain in the stationary phase longer.

Q12. After a much larger sample is injected, a chromatographic peak becomes broader and its retention time shifts. Which change is most likely to improve the chromatogram?

  1. Reduce the amount or concentration injected
  2. Inject twice as much sample
  3. Increase the number of reported decimal places
  4. Ignore the dead time
  5. Measure only the later part of the peak
Solution

A. Reduce the amount or concentration injected

The broader peak and retention shift after a larger injection indicate column overload or nonlinear partitioning. Reducing the injected mass should restore more nearly linear behavior and improve peak shape.

Q13. Walter White has decided to abandon his RV and become a chromatographer. Which phase carries his sample through the chromatographic system?

  1. The stationary phase
  2. The mobile phase
  3. The Batmobile phase
  4. His “Heisenberg phase”
  5. None of the above
Solution

B. The mobile phase

The mobile phase flows through the chromatographic system and transports the sample. The stationary phase remains fixed and interacts with analytes to produce differential retention.

Part B

1. During a field study, a solvent tank has spilled onto the surrounding soil. Visible staining extends downhill toward a drainage ditch.

(a) Which sampling strategy (judgmental or biased, simple random, or convenience) is most appropriate? Briefly explain how the locations would be chosen. (4 marks)

(b) Before moving to a new sampling location, what should the team do to the sampling tool so soil from the previous location does not enter the next sample? (2 marks)

Solution

(a) Use judgmental or biased sampling because the spill source and visible migration pathway are already known. Choose locations at the tank, throughout the visibly stained soil, along the downhill path toward the drainage ditch, and at the apparent edges of the affected area. Include suitable background or upgradient locations so the extent of contamination can be compared with unaffected soil.

(b) Decontaminate the sampling tool between locations. Remove adhering soil, wash or scrub the tool with the method-appropriate cleaning solution, rinse it as required, and use clean equipment or a clean tool surface for the next location. This prevents carryover and cross-contamination.

Mark allocation:

  • Appropriate judgmental or biased strategy: 1 mark
  • Locations target the source and visible/downhill migration path: 2 marks
  • Boundaries or background locations included: 1 mark
  • Proper tool decontamination between locations: 2 marks

2. A study is testing soil from a defined area for lead. The population is all soil within the chosen area and depth, but only 16 samples can be collected and analyzed. The mean of the 16 results is the sample mean.

(a) In your own words, explain the difference between the sample mean and the population mean. (2 marks)

(b) Which mean can be calculated directly from the 16 laboratory results? (1 mark)

(c) Why is the population mean for the whole area usually estimated from samples instead of measured directly? Give one practical reason and briefly explain it. (3 marks)

Solution

(a) The sample mean is the average of the 16 measured sample results. The population mean is the average concentration for all soil in the defined area and depth. The sample mean is used as an estimate of the unknown population mean.

(b) The sample mean can be calculated directly from the 16 laboratory results.

(c) Measuring every portion of soil in the population is impractical. It would require an enormous number of samples, excessive time and cost, and potentially destructive excavation of the entire area. A defensible sampling design provides a practical estimate of the population mean from a manageable subset.

  • Correct definition of sample mean: 1 mark
  • Correct definition of population mean: 1 mark
  • Identifies the sample mean: 1 mark
  • Gives a valid practical reason: 1 mark

4. Three solutes are analyzed using the same chromatographic column. Their retention factors are: solute A, k′ = 0.50; solute B, k′ = 2.0; and solute C, k′ = 5.0.

(a) Place the three solutes in the order they will elute from the column, from first to last. (3 marks)

(b) Explain why the solutes elute in this order. Relate each retention factor to the amount of time the solute spends in the stationary phase. (3 marks)

Solution

(a) First to last: A → B → C.

(b) A larger retention factor means that the solute spends more time in the stationary phase relative to the mobile phase and therefore elutes later.

  • Solute A, k'=0.50, spends the least relative time in the stationary phase and elutes first.
  • Solute B, k'=2.0, is retained more strongly and elutes second.
  • Solute C, k'=5.0, spends the greatest relative time in the stationary phase and elutes last.

Mark allocation:

  • Correct position for A: 1 mark
  • Correct position for B: 1 mark
  • Correct position for C: 1 mark
  • Connects larger k' with greater stationary-phase time and later elution: 3 marks

6. In a chromatographic run, a compound has a retention time of 8.00 minutes and a baseline peak width of 0.40 minutes on a 25.0 cm column. A second chromatographic system, operated under comparable conditions, has a plate height of 55 micrometres.

(a) Use the baseline peak-width equation to calculate the theoretical plate number (N) for the first system. (3 marks)

(b) Calculate the plate height (H) for the first system in micrometres. Then decide which system is more efficient and should produce narrower peaks. Explain your choice. (3 marks)

Solution

(a)

N=16(tRw)2 =16(8.000.40)2 =16(20.0)2 =6400

(b)

H=LN =25.0 cm6400 =0.00390625 cm

Using 1 cm=10,000 μm:

H=(0.00390625 cm)(10,000 μm/cm) =39.1 μm

The first system is more efficient because its plate height is approximately 39.1 μm, which is smaller than 55 μm. Under comparable conditions, a smaller plate height indicates less band broadening per unit column length and therefore narrower peaks.
