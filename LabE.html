Lab E. LC-RID

Extraction and determination of fructose and glucose in bell peppers

You will extract two sugars from a sweet bell pepper by solid-liquid extraction, then quantify them by HPLC with refractive index detection using the standard addition method, which corrects for the pepper's own matrix effects.

Concept overview

Solid-liquid extraction pulls a soluble component out of a solid using a solvent, the same idea as brewing coffee. Here, water pulls fructose and glucose out of chopped bell pepper, and the extract is then diluted with acetonitrile before analysis.

A pepper extract is a complex matrix, and that matrix can suppress or enhance the analyte signal compared to a clean standard, called a matrix effect. When standards cannot mimic the sample matrix closely enough, standard addition solves the problem: you spike several aliquots of the actual sample with increasing known amounts of analyte, plot signal versus concentration added, and the concentration already in the sample is the absolute value of the x-intercept (where the fitted line would cross zero signal), before accounting for any dilution.
