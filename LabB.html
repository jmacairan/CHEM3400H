Concept overview

Method validation confirms that an analytical method is accurate and reliable before you trust its numbers. Today's centerpiece is calibration: preparing standards of known concentration, measuring their signal, and fitting a line of signal versus concentration. A good calibration needs linearity (R² of 0.99 or better), and your unknown should fall in the middle of the standard range, not at the edges.

Beyond the curve itself, three QC checks matter: instrumental drift (rerun a mid-level standard at the end, compare by % change), calibration accuracy (does the rerun standard's calculated concentration match what you made?), and reference sample accuracy (% recovery on a sample of known concentration at several dilution levels). A control chart then tracks % recovery over many runs, with warning limits at 2 standard deviations and control limits at 3 standard deviations from the target.
