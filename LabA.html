Concept overview

Chromatography separates a mixture based on how each compound partitions between a stationary and a mobile phase. Every compound elutes with its own retention time. Peak area gives quantitative information (concentration), and retention time gives qualitative information (identity). Changing instrument parameters, such as oven temperature, flow rate, or a temperature ramp, changes how well compounds separate and how long the run takes.

Before trusting chromatographic data, you check system suitability: is the instrument separating things well enough, and consistently enough, to believe the numbers it reports? Today's three diagnostics are repeatability (%RSD), resolution between adjacent peaks, and peak asymmetry (tailing or fronting). You will run the same three-analyte mixture under three sets of conditions and judge which is best suited for this separation.
