Concept overview

Most real samples need preparation before analysis. Extraction transfers an analyte from one phase to another, often to concentrate it or separate it from interferences. In liquid-liquid extraction, two immiscible solvents (here, water and ethyl acetate) are shaken together so the analyte partitions into the phase you want to keep.

Today also introduces the internal standard method. A known amount of a different, chemically similar compound (diphenyl ether) is added to every sample and standard at the very start. Because the internal standard and the analyte experience the same extraction, injection, and instrument drift, plotting the ratio of analyte peak area to internal standard peak area cancels out a lot of that error. You will compare this to plain external calibration on a sample that was deliberately prepared incorrectly.
